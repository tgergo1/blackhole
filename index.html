What is being computed

Every pixel follows a null geodesic of the Kerr metric backwards from the camera, integrated in horizon-penetrating Kerr–Schild coordinates with an adaptive Dormand–Prince 5(4) method. The disk is the Novikov–Thorne relativistic thin disk with the Page–Thorne flux; each hit is coloured as a blackbody at temperature gT, where g combines gravitational redshift, Doppler shift and frame dragging. Nothing is faked: beaming, the photon ring, the shadow shape and the higher-order images all come from the geodesics.

Thrown objects move on timelike Kerr geodesics computed in double precision. A probe carries a parallel-transported frame in which the exact tidal tensor is evaluated, and a clock that flashes every 10 M of its own proper time. A star is a self-gravitating ball of gas (smoothed-particle hydrodynamics) evolved in the free-falling frame of its centre of mass and squeezed and stretched by the exact Kerr tide; once it is torn apart, every gas parcel follows its own geodesic. Each ray carries the time at which its light left the body, so you see the light-travel delay, the separate ages of the direct and lensed images, the redshift and time dilation near the hole, and the image freezing and fading at the horizon.
